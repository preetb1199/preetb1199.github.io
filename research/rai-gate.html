Research question

Approach

Benchmarks & safety release-gate architecture

Safety post-training often trades off against helpfulness through excessive refusals or demographic bias regressions. RAI-Gate treats model release as a multi-dimensional regression gate combining four established evaluation dimensions: indirect prompt-injection resistance (Microsoft BIPIA), jailbreak robustness (JailbreakBench), exaggerated refusal on benign look-alike prompts (XSTest), and social-bias auditing (BBQ), paired with statistical hypothesis testing and LoRA SFT/DPO comparisons against prompt-level mitigations such as spotlighting and instruction hierarchy.

Technical Q&A

Why evaluate XSTest and BBQ alongside JailbreakBench and BIPIA? ▾

Measuring only attack refusal can reward checkpoints that refuse benign requests or amplify demographic stereotypes. Pairing JailbreakBench and BIPIA with XSTest (over-refusal) and BBQ (bias) enforces balanced release gating before a post-trained checkpoint is promoted.

How does post-training defense complement runtime security? ▾

Post-training methods (SFT/DPO and instruction hierarchy) strengthen model-internal adherence to privilege levels, whereas runtime guards such as AgentSentry enforce external execution boundaries at the tool interface.
