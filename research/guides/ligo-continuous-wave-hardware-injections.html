Continuous-Wave Hardware Injection Monitoring in Advanced LIGO

Validating end-to-end detector calibration, phase coherence over 10¹¹ cycles, and microsecond timing accuracy during Observing Run 4

Unlike transient binary black-hole mergers lasting fractions of a second, continuous gravitational waves (CWs) from non-axisymmetric, rapidly spinning galactic neutron stars are persistent, nearly monochromatic signals buried deep in interferometer noise. Validating that Advanced LIGO hardware, calibration transfer functions, and search pipelines preserve amplitude, phase, and timing requires physical hardware injections monitored continuously throughout an observing run.

PUBLISHED CQG 2026
~44% Lower Uncertainty

Amplitude uncertainty reduction and 3–6 μs timing validation across 18 CW hardware injections (12–2,991 Hz).

1. Why physical hardware injections are irreplaceable

During LIGO's fourth observing run (O4), 18 simulated continuous-wave signals spanning approximately 12–2,991 Hz are physically actuated onto the interferometer test-mass mirrors via the detector actuation chain. Unlike software injections added after digitization, hardware injections traverse the complete physical optical cavity, sensing electronics, digital calibration filter, data-cleaning stages, and downstream astrophysical pipelines.

Because year-long coherent CW searches integrate phase over as many as 10¹¹ signal cycles, unmodeled timing offsets or phase drifts can rapidly degrade detection sensitivity—especially at kilohertz frequencies where a few microseconds corresponds to a measurable phase shift.

2. Comparing three O4 CW validation estimators (CQG 2026)

In Monitoring of continuous-wave hardware injections in LIGO interferometers during the O4 observing run (Baxi, Leviton, Morag, Pitkin & Riles, Classical and Quantum Gravity 43(13), 135019, July 2026), we compare three distinct estimation paradigms operating on daily and weekly timescales:

3. Signal-processing workflow: 1,800-second SFTs & inverse-variance weighting

The automated daily matched-filter pipeline ingests calibrated strain segmented into 1,800-second Short Fourier Transforms (SFTs), applies adaptive power windows around each known Doppler-modulated injection frequency track, estimates local background noise power spectral density, and combines segments via inverse-variance weighting. Running daily on the Caltech computing cluster allows rapid detection of calibration anomalies, missing injection actuation, or timing shifts without waiting for post-run offline analyses.

4. Semi-coherent CW search statistics & open Python tooling (CalculateSNRg_Python)

Beyond targeted hardware-injection monitoring, wide-parameter-space CW searches divide long observing runs of duration T_obs into N_seg = floor(T_obs / T_coh) coherent segments, choosing coherence time T_coh so that frequency spin-down ḟ drifts by roughly half a frequency bin per segment:

T_coh ≈ sqrt(0.5 / |ḟ|)

Our open-source repository CalculateSNRg_Python ↗ implements a grouped semi-coherent search simulation (PY_Calculate_SNRG.py + SNRgModelParams.csv) computing grouped signal-to-noise ratios (SNR_g for group sizes M ≤ g ≤ N), analytical noise mean/standard deviation, predicted signal detection statistics, and cubic-spline template spacings (scipy.interpolate.UnivariateSpline) identifying the step in Δf₀ or Δḟ that produces a 20% drop in SNR_g.
