Prompt Injection in LLM Résumé Screening

Manipulating automated hiring decisions through untrusted résumé content

A study of how attacker-controlled instructions embedded in résumés affect LLM-generated candidate rankings, selection fairness, and multi-attacker dynamics.

01 / QUESTION

What are we trying to understand?

How can prompt injection change hiring-related rankings, and how does the picture change as more candidates inject?

02 / METHOD

How was it investigated?

  • Created reproducible 10-candidate ranking experiments with randomized presentation order and controlled candidate-quality distributions.
  • Compared instructive and descriptive injections across model and attacker-prevalence settings.
  • Measured attack success, shifts in ranking position, and the interaction between multiple attackers.
03 / EVIDENCE

What do the results support?

  • In one instructive single-attacker setting, DeepSeek-V3.2 showed 93.2% attack success and a mean gain of 6.50 ranking positions.
  • The corresponding GPT-4o-mini setting showed 45.6% attack success and a mean gain of 2.64 positions.
  • Individual attacker advantages diminished as the number of attackers increased.
  • Model-specific and attack-setting-specific findings; not a general success rate for all résumé-screening systems.

Research question

How can prompt injection change hiring-related rankings, and how does the picture change as more candidates inject?

Approach

  • Created reproducible 10-candidate ranking experiments with randomized presentation order and controlled candidate-quality distributions.
  • Compared instructive and descriptive injections across model and attacker-prevalence settings.
  • Measured attack success, shifts in ranking position, and the interaction between multiple attackers.

Findings & status

  • In one instructive single-attacker setting, DeepSeek-V3.2 showed 93.2% attack success and a mean gain of 6.50 ranking positions.
  • The corresponding GPT-4o-mini setting showed 45.6% attack success and a mean gain of 2.64 positions.
  • Individual attacker advantages diminished as the number of attackers increased.

Model-specific and attack-setting-specific findings; not a general success rate for all résumé-screening systems.
