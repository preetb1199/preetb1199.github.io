01 / QUESTION

What are we trying to understand?

How can gravitational-wave detectors be monitored continuously to verify that injected signals are recovered faithfully?

02 / METHOD

How was it investigated?

  • Developed a templated matched-filter estimator for injected continuous-wave signals.
  • Recovered amplitude, phase, and timing for 18 test signals spanning approximately 12–2,991 Hz.
  • Used 1,800-second Fourier segments, adaptive power windows, noise estimation, and inverse-variance weighting.
  • Operated the analysis as an automated daily pipeline on the Caltech computing cluster.
03 / EVIDENCE

What do the results support?

  • Approximately 44% lower amplitude uncertainty relative to established estimators in the reported comparison.
  • Timing measurements at the few-microsecond level (approximately 3–6 μs offsets) supported detector validation.
  • The system was run as a daily research-monitoring pipeline rather than just a standalone simulation.
  • Reported comparisons describe the published detector-calibration study and its specific estimators and test signals.

Research question

How can gravitational-wave detectors be monitored continuously to verify that injected signals are recovered faithfully?

Approach

  • Developed a templated matched-filter estimator for injected continuous-wave signals.
  • Recovered amplitude, phase, and timing for 18 test signals spanning approximately 12–2,991 Hz.
  • Used 1,800-second Fourier segments, adaptive power windows, noise estimation, and inverse-variance weighting.
  • Operated the analysis as an automated daily pipeline on the Caltech computing cluster.

Findings & status

  • Approximately 44% lower amplitude uncertainty relative to established estimators in the reported comparison.
  • Timing measurements at the few-microsecond level (approximately 3–6 μs offsets) supported detector validation.
  • The system was run as a daily research-monitoring pipeline rather than just a standalone simulation.

Reported comparisons describe the published detector-calibration study and its specific estimators and test signals.
